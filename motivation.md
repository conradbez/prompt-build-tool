LLMs is the drunken tourist in Dublin.

On a trip through Dublin,a friend and I were walking back from a pub after a few pints, talking. I had Google Maps on and was laser focussed on following the map in a strange city knowing I'm tipsy. To my horror, after 30min of walking my friend asks "isn't this the pub we came from".  In part due to conversation, in part due to the beers I had turned around and started following the "this is where you have walked" part of the map. I wished I had the common sense to check my suroundings along the way instead of blindly following phones guide on the path to follow. Similarly, I think when people develop software, they should be thinking about "landmarks", "directionality" and "smoke tests" to test direction and accuracy along the way. And LLM's too.

I think we have a intuitive sense that it we need to work off a map, and we need to validate our map's effectiveness against the real world.

Check your intuition against reality.

Landmarks: known-good states you can recognise independently of the tooling.

These checks only help if you make a prediction first. "I expect to see the river on my left" is a test of your intuition; glancing at the blue dot isn't.

When reality and the map disagree, update the map.

On their own, they can't glance up and notice "that's the pub again."
