Tidal energy is a form of renewable power generated by the natural rise and fall of ocean tides, which are driven by the gravitational pull of the moon and the sun. As vast amounts of water move toward and away from shorelines, the resulting currents carry immense kinetic and potential energy. Specialized equipment, such as underwater turbines and tidal barrages, captures this hydrodynamic movement and converts it into clean, usable electricity for the power grid.

One of the greatest advantages of tidal energy is its exceptional predictability, as ocean tides follow precise, immutable schedules unlike the variable patterns of wind and solar power. However, the widespread adoption of tidal technology faces challenges, including high initial construction costs, limited geographic locations with strong enough tides, and potential environmental impacts on local marine ecosystems. Despite these hurdles, advancements in marine engineering continue to make tidal energy an increasingly viable contributor to the global shift toward sustainable power.
