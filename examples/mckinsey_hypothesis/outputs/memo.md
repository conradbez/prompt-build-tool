# Heat-pump installation financing for landlords

**Answer:** The client should pivot the launch to a broader residential heat-pump financing platform with a specialized landlord module, as the exclusive landlord market is currently too small to meet the £150M origination target by 2027.
**Recommendation:** go with conditions

## Situation, complication, question
- **Situation:** Governments are accelerating residential decarbonization mandates to meet net-zero targets, positioning heat pumps as the primary alternative to fossil fuel heating. The rental sector represents a critical portion of the housing stock, yet these properties remain largely un-retrofitted due to high upfront capital requirements.
- **Complication:** Tightening energy efficiency regulations are forcing landlords to upgrade, but the 'split-incentive' problem and high interest rates have created a significant financing gap. Current lending products do not account for the specific cash-flow profiles of landlords or the potential for green-asset value appreciation, leaving a massive underserved market.
- **Key question:** Should the client launch a dedicated heat-pump financing product for the UK residential landlord market to achieve £150M in annual loan originations and a 15% Return on Equity (ROE) by 2027?

## Governing hypothesis
- **Going in:** The client should launch the dedicated heat-pump financing product because the UK's transition to Net Zero creates a multi-billion pound addressable market among landlords where a partnership-led distribution model will secure the required 15% market share at a 15% ROE.
- **Coming out (revised):** The client should launch a multi-segment heat-pump financing product targeting the 'long tail' of MCS installers, using a specialized tax-deductible loan structure for landlords to capture a high-margin niche while relying on the broader residential market to reach the £150M volume target.

## Supporting arguments
1. The addressable landlord-only financing market is capped at £80M-£100M annually due to the £7,500 BUS grant reducing principal needs and competition from low-cost mortgage top-ups.
2. A 15% ROE is achievable by leveraging the lower default profiles of property owners and a low-cost (<5% CAC) distribution model through a white-label platform for independent installers.
3. The re-introduction of EPC 'C' mandates will drive landlord demand, but reaching the £150M target requires capturing volume from the wider homeowner market where installation rates are higher.

## Biggest risk
The £7,500 Boiler Upgrade Scheme (BUS) grant significantly reduces the required loan principal per installation, making the £150M origination target highly dependent on massive unit volume that current UK installation rates cannot support.

## Next steps
- Run a 4-week pilot with 10 'Long Tail' MCS installers to validate the conversion rate of a landlord-specific 'tax-deductible' loan offer.
- Survey 500 residential homeowners to determine the interest rate sensitivity of switching from a mortgage top-up to a dedicated heat-pump loan.
- Model the impact on ROE if the £7,500 BUS grant is phased out, increasing average loan size but potentially lowering overall market demand.
