# What grabs and holds a South African audience

I don't have your data, so this is a framework with hypotheses to test, not findings.

## 1. Context that shapes attention

| Factor | Implication |
|---|---|
| **Mobile-first, expensive data** | Audiences favor platforms with data-saving options. Downloads and offline listening matter, and long video streams cost money. Watch download vs. stream split. |
| **WhatsApp as the main sharing channel** | Much sharing is invisible "dark social." Use UTM links or short links to measure it. |
| **YouTube dominance** | Long-form conversational shows often do well on YouTube, with Spotify growing. Apple Podcasts is less dominant than in the US. |
| **Commuting (taxis, traffic) and load shedding** | Listening peaks may follow commute windows and outage patterns, and audio-only works well in these settings. |
| **Multilingual, code-switching** | English is the common denominator, but code-switching (Zulu, Xhosa, Afrikaans, Sotho, slang) can signal authenticity and boost engagement. |
| **Strong radio heritage** | Talk radio expectations (personality-led, call-in, news-driven) carry over to podcasts. |

## 2. Topics likely to resonate (hypotheses)

- **Cost of living and money:** fuel, electricity, side hustles, debt, SASSA, investing, the rand
- **Safety and crime:** true crime, community policing, scams
- **Governance and accountability:** corruption, service delivery, Eskom, water, GNU politics
- **Jobs and opportunity:** unemployment, entrepreneurship, emigration or "semigration"
- **Sport:** Springboks, Bafana Bafana, PSL (Chiefs, Pirates, Sundowns)
- **Culture:** amapiano, comedy, relationships, faith, identity, language
- **Local personalities and controversy:** "hot take" and celebrity content tends to spike clicks, though not always retention

Test these. Don't assume them. Tag every episode by topic so you can compare.

## 3. Metrics that signal attention

**Hooks (grabbing)**
- **Click-through rate** on title and thumbnail
- **Retention at 5s, 15s, and 30s** (the early drop-off cliff)
- **Hook rate** on short clips (3-second views ÷ impressions)
- **Start rate** on audio (plays that last more than 60 seconds)

**Holding**
- **Average percentage viewed or listened**, not just average duration
- **Retention curve shape:** a steady decline is normal, while spikes and dips show the moments that matter. Rewatch spikes mean high-value moments, and sharp drops show where you lose people (long intros, ad breaks, tangents).
- **Completion rate** by episode length bucket

**Format and length**
- Compare **percentage retained** across length buckets (under 15, 15–30, 30–60, 60+ min). Absolute minutes consumed can mislead, because a 90-minute episode with 35% retention still delivers more minutes than a 20-minute one with 70%.
- Compare formats (solo, interview, panel, call-in, narrative, clips) while controlling for topic.

**Loyalty and spread**
- **Returning vs. new viewers**
- **Subscribe or follow conversion per episode**
- **Shares, saves, and comments** (comment language and sentiment are useful qualitative data)
- **Episode-to-episode drop-off:** do people continue to the next one?

**Audience**
- City and province, device, time of day, language, age

## 4. How to read the signals

- **High CTR + low retention:** the hook over-promises (clickbait). Fix the delivery.
- **Low CTR + high retention:** great content, weak packaging. Fix titles and thumbnails.
- **Strong early retention + mid-episode cliff:** the structure is the problem (ad load, rambling).
- **High shares + moderate retention:** a "talkable" topic. It is good for growth, not necessarily depth.
- **Strong retention in long episodes:** the audience wants depth and conversation, so don't cut for length alone.

## 5. Pitfalls

- Guest popularity can confound topic and format analysis.
- Small samples and a few viral outliers distort averages, so use medians.
- Platform data differs (Spotify, YouTube, and Apple define "plays" differently).
- Controversy spikes engagement but may harm trust or brand fit.

## 6. A practical test plan

1. Tag the last 20–50 episodes by topic, format, length, guest, hook style, and language mix.
2. Pull CTR, 30-second retention, average percentage viewed, shares, and follower conversion.
3. Rank by retention-adjusted engagement, not raw views.
4. Run controlled tests: same topic with different hooks, or same format with different lengths.
5. Review comments in the languages your audience uses.
