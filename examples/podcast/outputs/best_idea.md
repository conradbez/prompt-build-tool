# Pick: #5 The Money Minute (Plus Four)

## Why it fits best

- **Topic match:** Cost of living and money lead the research's list of likely-resonant topics (fuel, electricity, debt, side hustles, SASSA, investing, the rand). Most other ideas, like weird history, literary readings, and mindfulness, aren't on the list at all.
- **Format match:** A ~5-minute audio episode is cheap to download, easy to save for offline listening, and works in a taxi or in traffic. That suits the mobile-first, expensive-data, commute-and-load-shedding context.
- **Shareability:** A single action ("check your credit score today") is easy to forward on WhatsApp, where most sharing happens. You can measure this with short links or UTMs.
- **Sustainability:** A daily single-host show risks burnout. Personal finance is mostly evergreen, so it can be batch-recorded. A news show can't be, because it goes stale.
- **Retention:** The "one small action" ending gives listeners a reason to return and gives you something to measure, such as episode-to-episode drop-off and follower conversion.

## Why not the others

- **#1 The First Ten** is the strongest runner-up. It matches the radio heritage and commute habit, but it competes with free radio and news apps. It also can't be batched, so a solo host would have to produce it fresh every day.
- **#8 The Local Loop** touches governance and service delivery, but it's too narrow and labor-intensive for a solo daily show.
- **#3, #4, #6, #7 and #9** are pleasant but generic, and nothing in the research suggests demand for them. #9 could work with local stories, but it isn't a hypothesized priority topic.
- **#2 and #10** are fun, but they're weaker on topic fit. #10's interactivity also depends on a community you'd have to build first.

## What needs to change

1. **Localize completely.** Use rands, SA tax-free savings accounts, and local banks, plus real SA situations like fuel price changes, electricity costs, and side hustles. Generic or US-centric explainers would undercut the whole advantage.
2. **Make it personality-led.** The research points to radio-style expectations, so a distinct host voice matters. Consider light code-switching if it feels natural, not forced.
3. **Add occasional timely hooks,** such as the monthly fuel price announcement or a rand move, to get some of the news-show pull while staying mostly evergreen.
4. **Include a "not financial advice" note,** since you'll be telling people what to do with their money.

## Caveat

The research is a framework with hypotheses, not data, so this is the best-supported bet, not a sure thing. I'd launch it with tagged episodes (topic, hook style, length, language mix) and track 30-second retention, average percentage listened, shares, and follower conversion. If The First Ten-style timeliness outperforms, you can shift toward it.
